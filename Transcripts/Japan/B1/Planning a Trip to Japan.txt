Sam: I definitely recommend avoiding the summer months like July and August. It is very hot and humid, which makes walking around the cities quite tiring.

Sam: Tokyo is amazing. It feels like the future with all the neon lights and tall buildings. You have to visit Shibuya to see the famous busy crossing. But Tokyo also has a quiet side. You can visit the Meiji Shrine, which is located in a big forest right in the middle of the city.

Alex: After Tokyo, you should head toward nature. A great stop is Hakone. It is only a short train ride away. Hakone is famous for its hot springs and views of Mount Fuji. If the weather is clear, seeing the mountain is a real highlight of the trip.

Sam: From Hakone, you can take the Shinkansen, which is the famous Japanese bullet train. It is very fast and comfortable. You should take it to Kyoto.

Alex: Kyoto is the cultural heart of Japan. While Tokyo is modern, Kyoto feels like stepping back in time. There are thousands of temples and beautiful traditional gardens. You must visit the Kinkaku-ji, also known as the Golden Pavilion.

Sam: And don't forget the Arashiyama Bamboo Grove! Walking through the tall, green bamboo is a very peaceful experience. It’s one of the best places for nature lovers.

Alex: While you are in that area, you should take a day trip to Nara. It is very close to Kyoto and it’s famous for its park where hundreds of deer walk around freely. They are very friendly, and you can even buy crackers to feed them.

Sam: To finish the trip, I recommend spending the last two days in Osaka. It is only thirty minutes from Kyoto. Osaka is known as "Japan’s Kitchen" because the street food is incredible. You have to try Takoyaki, which are small dough balls with octopus inside.
