Second, speaking of size, the Big Island is truly enormous. It covers about 4,028 square miles, making it larger than all the other Hawaiian islands combined. That's a lot of space to explore!

Third fact: the Big Island is home to Mauna Kea, one of the world's tallest mountains. If you measure from the ocean floor to the summit, it's actually taller than Mount Everest! Many scientists use Mauna Kea for astronomical observations because it's so high and far from city lights.

Now, here's something really interesting. The Big Island has eleven different climate zones. This means you can experience completely different weather in different parts of the island. You might see snow on the mountains while enjoying sunshine on the beaches just a few hours away.

Fifth, the island is home to Hawaii Volcanoes National Park, which is absolutely spectacular. This park protects two of the world's most active volcanoes: Kilauea and Mauna Loa. Visitors can see volcanic craters, lava tubes, and stunning geological formations.

Sixth interesting fact: the Big Island produces the most macadamia nuts in the world. These delicious nuts are a major agricultural product, and you'll find them everywhere on the island. Many visitors love buying fresh macadamia nuts to take home.

Here's number seven: the Big Island has some of the most beautiful beaches in Hawaii. From black sand beaches created by lava to white sand and even green sand beaches, the variety is incredible. Each beach has its own unique character and beauty.

Eighth fact: Kona coffee is world-famous, and it grows only in the Kona district on the Big Island's western coast. The volcanic soil and perfect climate create ideal conditions for growing this high-quality coffee. If you visit, you must try authentic Kona coffee!

Number nine: the Big Island is the only Hawaiian island where you can see snow regularly. During winter months, snow falls on the summits of Mauna Kea and Mauna Loa. It's quite unusual to think about snow in Hawaii, right?

Finally, our tenth fact: the Big Island is home to the Punalu'u Black Sand Beach, one of the most photographed beaches in Hawaii. This beach is famous for its black sand and for the endangered green sea turtles that rest there. It's a truly magical place to visit.
